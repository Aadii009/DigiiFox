      UNDERSTANDING THE MATH

      What is a prime number?

      A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 that has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. For example, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13 are prime numbers.

      A number greater than 1 that has more than two positive factors is called a composite number. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.

      COMMON QUESTIONS

      Frequently asked questions

      Is 1 a prime number?

      No. A prime number must have exactly two distinct positive factors. The number 1 has only one positive factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.

      Why is 2 the only even prime number?

      Every even whole number greater than 2 is divisible by 2 and itself, so it has more than two positive factors. The number 2 has exactly two: 1 and 2.

      How does the calculator check a number?

      It tests possible divisors up to the square root of the number. If no integer divisor is found in that range, the number is prime when it is greater than 1.

      What is prime factorization?

      Prime factorization expresses a positive integer greater than 1 as a product of prime numbers. For example, 12 = 2 × 2 × 3.

      Can I find every prime number in a range?

      Yes. Choose Find primes in a range and enter the start and end values. The range can contain up to 100,000 integers.

      Are my calculations processed online?

      The calculations run locally in your browser. The calculator does not need to upload your entered numbers to calculate the results.
