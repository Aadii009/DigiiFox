UNDERSTANDING MARKUP

What Is a Markup Calculator?

A Markup Calculator helps businesses, retailers and sellers determine how much to add to the cost of a product to establish its selling price.

Markup is expressed as an amount or as a percentage of the cost price. For example, if an item costs ₹1,000 and is sold for ₹1,500, the markup is ₹500, or 50% of the cost.

Markup and profit margin are not the same. A 50% markup on cost produces a 33.33% profit margin when the selling price is ₹1,500 and the cost is ₹1,000.

THE MATHEMATICS

Markup Calculator Formulas

Learn how markup, selling price and margin are calculated.

01

Markup Amount

Selling Price − Cost Price

The difference between the selling price and the cost price.

02

Markup Percentage

(Markup ÷ Cost Price) × 100

Shows markup relative to the original cost.

03

Profit Margin

(Markup ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Shows the profit as a percentage of sales.

04

Selling Price

Cost × (1 + Markup % ÷ 100)

Calculates a selling price from a target markup.

05

Cost Price

Selling Price ÷ (1 + Markup % ÷ 100)

Finds the cost price when selling price and markup percentage are known.

06

Cost Recovery

(Cost ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Shows how much of the selling price represents the cost.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about markup calculations.

What is markup percentage?

Markup percentage measures the difference between selling price and cost price as a percentage of cost price.

What is the difference between markup and profit margin?

Markup is calculated using cost as the base, while profit margin uses selling price. For a ₹1,000 cost and ₹1,500 selling price, markup is 50% and profit margin is 33.33%.

How do I calculate a selling price with a 40% markup?

Multiply the cost price by 1.40. For example, ₹1,000 × 1.40 = ₹1,400.

Can markup percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. If the markup amount exceeds the cost price, the markup percentage is greater than 100%. For example, a ₹1,000 cost and ₹2,500 selling price produce a 150% markup.

Can a product have a negative markup?

Yes. If the selling price is lower than the cost price, the difference is negative. This indicates a loss rather than a positive markup.

Are my calculations stored or uploaded?

No. Calculations are performed locally in your browser. This calculator does not need to send your entered values to a server.
