UNDERSTANDING THE BASICS

What Is a Profit & Loss Calculator?

A Profit & Loss Calculator helps you determine whether a product or transaction generates a profit or a loss. It compares the selling price with the total cost, including additional expenses.

If the selling price is higher than the total cost, you earn a profit. If it is lower, you experience a loss. If both values are equal, you break even.

THE MATHEMATICS

Profit & Loss Formulas

Understand how each financial result is calculated.

01

Total Cost

Cost Price + Additional Expenses

The total amount spent to purchase and prepare a product for sale.

02

Profit

Selling Price − Total Cost

Profit occurs when the selling price exceeds the total cost.

03

Loss

Total Cost − Selling Price

Loss occurs when the total cost exceeds the selling price.

04

Profit Percentage

(Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100

Measures profit relative to the total cost.

05

Loss Percentage

(Loss ÷ Total Cost) × 100

Measures loss relative to the total cost.

06

Profit Margin

(Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Shows the percentage of sales revenue retained as profit.

07

Markup Percentage

(Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100

Shows profit as a percentage of the total cost.

08

Break-Even Point

Selling Price = Total Cost

You neither make a profit nor experience a loss when both amounts are equal.

WORKED EXAMPLE

How Is Profit Calculated?

Suppose you purchase a product for ₹1,000, spend another ₹100 on expenses and sell it for ₹1,500.

  • Cost Price ₹1,000
  • Additional Expenses ₹100
  • Total Cost ₹1,100
  • Selling Price ₹1,500
  • Net Profit ₹400
✓
Profit Percentage 36.36%

₹400 ÷ ₹1,100 × 100

Profit Margin 26.67%

₹400 ÷ ₹1,500 × 100

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn more about profit, loss and related calculations.

What is the difference between profit and revenue?

Revenue is the amount received from sales before deducting costs. Profit is what remains after subtracting the total costs from revenue.

How do I calculate profit percentage?

Divide profit by the total cost and multiply the result by 100. For example, a ₹400 profit on a total cost of ₹1,100 equals approximately 36.36%.

What is the difference between profit margin and markup?

Markup measures profit against total cost. Profit margin measures profit against selling price. Both are percentages, but they use different denominators.

Are additional expenses included in the calculation?

Yes. Enter your additional expenses in the calculator. They are added to the purchase cost before profit, loss and related percentages are calculated.

What happens when selling price equals total cost?

You break even. Your profit and loss are both zero, and there is no profit or loss percentage to calculate for that transaction.

Are my financial details uploaded anywhere?

No. This calculator performs calculations directly in your browser. It does not need to upload your entered financial values to a server.
