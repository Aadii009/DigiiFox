    UNDERSTANDING THE MATH

    What are LCM and HCF?

    LCM and HCF are mathematical concepts used to find relationships between whole numbers. They are useful in fractions, number patterns, division, and many everyday mathematical problems.

    LCM

    Least Common Multiple

    The LCM is the smallest positive number that is divisible by every number in the given set.

    Example: LCM of 4 and 6 = 12
    HCF

    Highest Common Factor

    The HCF is the largest positive whole number that divides every number in the given set without leaving a remainder.

    Example: HCF of 12 and 18 = 6

    Important formulas

    For two positive integers: LCM(a, b) = (a × b) ÷ HCF(a, b)
    Euclidean algorithm for HCF: HCF(a, b) = HCF(b, a mod b)
    For more than two numbers: LCM(a, b, c) = LCM(LCM(a, b), c)

    COMMON QUESTIONS

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between LCM and HCF?

    LCM is the smallest positive common multiple of the given numbers. HCF is the largest positive integer that divides all the given numbers without a remainder.

    Are HCF and GCD the same?

    Yes. HCF means Highest Common Factor, while GCD means Greatest Common Divisor. Both refer to the same mathematical value.

    Can I calculate LCM and HCF for more than two numbers?

    Yes. Enter between 2 and 20 positive whole numbers, separated by commas or spaces.

    Can I enter decimals or negative numbers?

    No. This calculator accepts positive whole numbers only. Decimals, negative values, zero, and non-numeric entries are not accepted.

    What happens when the numbers are coprime?

    If two numbers have an HCF of 1, they are coprime. Their LCM equals their product. For example, the LCM of 5 and 7 is 35.
