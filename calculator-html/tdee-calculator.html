What Is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) estimates the energy your body uses over a typical day. It includes resting metabolism, movement, exercise, and the energy used to digest and process food. A calculator estimates this value; it does not directly measure your metabolism.

TDEE Formula

This calculator first estimates Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. It then multiplies BMR by an activity factor to estimate TDEE.

Step 1: Estimate BMR — male equation BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
Step 1: Estimate BMR — female equation BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161
Step 2: Estimate TDEE TDEE ≈ BMR × activity factor

Activity Levels Explained

Activity level Factor Typical description
Sedentary 1.20 Little exercise; mostly sitting
Lightly active 1.375 Some light exercise or movement
Moderately active 1.55 Regular moderate exercise
Very active 1.725 Frequent hard exercise or an active routine
Extra active 1.90 Very demanding training or physical work

These are approximate conventional multipliers. Real energy expenditure varies between people, and activity categories do not precisely predict individual calorie needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates energy used at rest. TDEE estimates total daily energy expenditure, including activity. TDEE is generally higher than BMR.

How accurate is this TDEE calculator?

It provides an estimate based on a predictive equation and an activity multiplier. Actual needs can differ because of body composition, routine, health, and individual metabolic variation.

What are maintenance calories?

Maintenance calories are an estimate of the energy needed to maintain body weight over time, assuming activity and other relevant factors remain broadly stable.

Should I eat exactly the number shown?

Not necessarily. Treat the result as a starting estimate. Actual energy needs may differ, and individual nutrition advice should account for your health, goals, and circumstances.

Can TDEE change over time?

Yes. Changes in body weight, exercise, everyday movement, age, and other factors can change your energy needs.
