UNDERSTAND THE MATH

Compound Interest Formula

Compound interest is calculated on the original principal and the interest accumulated in previous compounding periods.

Final Amount A = P(1 + r/n)nt
Compound Interest CI = A − P
  • A: Final amount
  • P: Initial principal
  • r: Annual interest rate as a decimal
  • n: Compounding periods per year
  • t: Time in years

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn the basics of compound interest.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on the original principal and the interest accumulated during earlier compounding periods.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal alone. Compound interest also takes previously accumulated interest into account.

Which compounding frequency should I select?

Select the frequency specified by your investment or loan terms. More frequent compounding generally produces a higher final balance for a positive nominal rate, when the rate and time remain the same.

Does monthly compounding mean monthly deposits?

No. Monthly compounding means interest is applied monthly. This calculator models one initial lump-sum amount and does not include recurring deposits.

How are months and days calculated?

Months are converted using 12 months per year, and days using 365 days per year. The formula allows fractional compounding periods for an estimate. Financial institutions may use different conventions.

Is the result guaranteed investment growth?

No. The result assumes a fixed interest rate and the selected compounding schedule. It does not include fees, taxes, market changes, or other investment-specific conditions.
