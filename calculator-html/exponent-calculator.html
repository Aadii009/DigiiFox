    UNDERSTANDING EXPONENTS

    What Is an Exponent?

    An exponent tells you how many times a base is multiplied by itself when the exponent is a positive whole number.

    Basic Formula
    xy

    x = base, y = exponent, and xy = power or result.

    Example: 2³

    In this expression, 2 is the base and 3 is the exponent. Multiply 2 by itself three times:

    2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

    Important Exponent Rules

    Positive exponent 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
    Zero exponent x⁰ = 1, when x ≠ 0
    Negative exponent x⁻ⁿ = 1 / xⁿ, when x ≠ 0
    Fractional exponent x¹ᐟ² = √x for x ≥ 0

    Note: 0⁰ is undefined. A negative base raised to a non-integer exponent may not have a real result. This calculator reports an error when the requested result is not a real number.

    COMMON QUESTIONS

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What does an exponent mean?

    An exponent indicates a power. For a positive whole-number exponent, it tells you how many times to multiply the base by itself.

    What is 2 raised to the power of 3?

    2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

    What happens when the exponent is zero?

    Any nonzero base raised to the power of zero equals 1. The expression 0⁰ is undefined.

    Can I calculate a negative exponent?

    Yes. For example, 2⁻³ = 1 / 2³ = 1 / 8 = 0.125. The base cannot be zero when the exponent is negative.

    Can I use decimal exponents?

    Yes. For example, 9⁰·⁵ = √9 = 3. Some combinations of negative bases and non-integer exponents do not produce real numbers.
